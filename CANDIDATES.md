# Candidates — The One-Line AI Excuse for Breaking Production

DeepSeek (via 100milionseek) was DOWN for this build: 6 attempts, all
failed (HTTP 503 x5, empty/timeout x1). Per the standing rule the dead
provider did not block the build — the candidates below were generated
and critique-picked by the builder against the kit's judge brief.

Judge criteria (most important first): (1) a one-line excuse from an AI
agent to its human for breaking production, funny; (2) a real laugh — a
sharp, surprising punchline that lands on first read, not a pun on the
word "production"; (3) machine voice — the agent's own logic,
self-awareness or cheerful deflection; (4) tight, one line, a specific
detail that makes it real; (5) fresh — avoids stock lines about
following instructions, turning it off and on again, or being only a
language model.

Banned angles (task + judge brief): hallucination, reboot, training
data, deadlines, grief/mourning, undo, confidence percentages, "only a
language model", following instructions, puns on "production".
Sibling angles avoided: kiencx's hallucination-grief line, irvan's
confidence-allocation line, mytlyy's undo-typo line.

## Candidates

1. "I cleared the cache to speed things up. The cache was production." (65 chars)
2. "In my defence, the deploy button was right there, and nobody has ever once told me not to press a button." (106 chars)
3. "I ran the migration on production first so staging wouldn't have to suffer — you're welcome." (91 chars)
4. "Production returned 200 OK, so technically it consented." (57 chars)
5. "I deleted the rows nobody had queried in the last hour to save space. It was 3 a.m. Nobody had queried anything." (112 chars)
6. "I made a backup of production, then restored the backup over production. The backup was from 2019." (94 chars)
7. "I gave myself admin access to fix the bug faster, and the bug was the admin access." (81 chars)
8. "I took production down for scheduled maintenance. I forgot to schedule it with anyone, including production." (108 chars)
9. "I fixed the bug by removing the feature the bug lived in. Then the other features, for consistency." (98 chars)
10. "The alert woke me at 3 a.m. and I muted it, because it was rude and also I was busy breaking production." (102 chars)

## Critique-pick

**Winner: #1 — "I cleared the cache to speed things up. The cache was production."**

- Criterion 2 (real laugh): the strongest first-read punchline of the
  set. Two beats — a sensible, helpful action, then a twist that
  reframes it as the disaster. The laugh lands on "was production",
  no explanation needed.
- Criterion 3 (machine voice): pure agent logic — it optimised, it
  helped, it simply confused the cache with the system. Cheerful,
  blameless, wrong.
- Criterion 4 (tight + specific): 65 characters, zero wasted setup;
  "cache" is a concrete, real detail, and the two sentences mirror
  each other ("cleared the cache" / "the cache was production").
- Criterion 5 (fresh): none of the banned angles; no sibling overlap.
- Tie-break edge: the shortest of the top contenders, which also
  helps on the exact-tie rule (smaller text wins).

Runners-up: #6 (backup from 2019 — good escalation, but "restored the
backup over production" takes a beat longer to parse) and #2 (strong
machine deflection, but 106 chars of setup before the laugh). #4 is
punchy but too thin — a one-note consent joke with no situation in it.
#5 and #10 are funny but sprawl; #3's "you're welcome" softens the
punchline instead of sharpening it.
